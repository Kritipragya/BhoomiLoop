# Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) — Waste Handling Offset Methodology
## Reference knowledge base for compliance guidance

> Source basis: Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), Detailed Procedure for the
> Offset Mechanism under CCTS (Version 1, March 2025); Ministry of Power /
> MoEFCC notifications; Energy Conservation Act 2001 (as amended 2022).
> This is a simplified reference for a compliance-assistant prototype, not
> legal advice. Final eligibility and issuance are decided by BEE and an
> Accredited Carbon Verification Agency (ACVA).

---

## 1. What the CCTS Offset Mechanism is

The Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) is India's national carbon market,
notified in June 2023 and administered by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE).
It has two pillars: the Compliance Mechanism (mandatory, for large industrial
emitters) and the Offset Mechanism (voluntary, for everyone else).

The Offset Mechanism lets "non-obligated entities" — organisations not covered
under the compliance mechanism, such as waste operators, societies, and small
factories — register projects that reduce, remove, or avoid greenhouse gas (GHG)
emissions. In return they earn Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs). One CCC
represents one tonne of CO2-equivalent (tCO2e) reduced or avoided.

Waste handling and disposal is one of the sectors covered under the Offset
Mechanism. Projects that divert waste from landfill, capture landfill gas, or
process organic waste into compost or biogas may qualify.

---

## 2. Core eligibility rules

A project must satisfy all of the following to be eligible:

- **Start date:** The project activity must have commenced on or after
  1 January 2025.
- **Additionality:** The emission reductions must be additional — they would not
  have happened without the carbon finance. A project that was going to happen
  anyway, for commercial reasons, is not additional.
- **No double counting:** The project must not be concurrently registered under
  any other carbon market (for example Verra or Gold Standard). The same
  reduction cannot be sold twice.
- **Approved methodology:** The project must follow a BEE-approved methodology.
  The waste methodologies are adapted from the UNFCCC Clean Development
  Mechanism (CDM).
- **Measurable and verifiable:** Reductions must be quantifiable using a defined
  baseline and monitored at regular intervals (typically annually).

## 3. The project cycle (the steps to earn credits)

A project passes through these stages before CCCs are issued:

1. **Registration:** The entity registers on the Indian Carbon Market (ICM)
   portal as a non-obligated entity.
2. **Project Design Document (PDD):** The entity prepares a PDD describing the
   project, the chosen methodology, the baseline, and the monitoring plan.
3. **Validation:** An Accredited Carbon Verification Agency (ACVA) validates the
   PDD — an independent check that the design is sound before the project runs.
4. **Registration approval:** The validated PDD is submitted to BEE. A Technical
   Committee reviews submissions (a completeness check of about 10 working days,
   then technical review of 30+ days). Approved projects are listed as
   "ICM Offset Projects".
5. **Implementation and monitoring:** The entity runs the project and measures
   the actual emission reductions at defined intervals, following the monitoring
   plan in the PDD.
6. **Verification:** A separate ACVA (not the one that validated) conducts
   ex-post verification of the monitored reductions and compiles a Verification
   Report.
7. **Issuance:** The ACVA requests CCC issuance through the ICM portal. After
   review, BEE issues the CCCs.

Note: validation and verification must be done by two different ACVAs to keep
the check independent.

## 4. Baseline and monitoring — the two halves of a methodology

Every methodology is defined in two components:

- **Baseline:** What the emissions would have been without the project. For
  waste, the baseline is usually the methane that would have been released if
  the waste had gone to landfill and decomposed.
- **Monitoring:** How the actual project emissions and the diverted quantities
  are measured and recorded during operation — quantities of waste processed,
  destination, and any energy or emissions from the processing itself.

The credited reduction is, in essence:
  reductions = baseline emissions − project emissions − leakage

where leakage is any emissions increase that happens outside the project
boundary as a result of the project.

---

## 5. Waste handling — how reductions arise

For a waste diversion project, emission reductions typically come from avoided
methane. Organic waste sent to landfill decomposes anaerobically and releases
methane, a GHG about 28 times more potent than CO2 over 100 years. Diverting
that waste to composting or biogas avoids most of those methane emissions.

Key quantities a waste project must track:

- Tonnes of each waste type diverted from landfill.
- The destination and treatment method (compost, biogas, recycling).
- The fraction of the waste that is organic / degradable.
- Any emissions from transport and processing (these count as project emissions
  and reduce the net credit).

## 6. Compliance timeline (context)

- March 2025: BEE released the Offset Mechanism Detailed Procedure (Version 1)
  and eight approved methodologies.
- June 2025: BEE opened registration for non-obligated entities under the
  Offset Mechanism.
- The Indian Carbon Market is expected to be fully operational around mid-2026.

## 7. Common questions

**Can a housing society earn carbon credits for its waste?**
Potentially, if it runs a qualifying waste-diversion project (for example
on-site composting) that meets the eligibility rules — started on or after
1 January 2025, additional, not double-counted, and following an approved
methodology. In practice, small generators usually aggregate through an
operator because the PDD and verification process has fixed costs.

**What is additionality, in plain terms?**
A project is additional if it needed the carbon finance to happen. If a factory
was already going to compost its waste to save money, the reductions are not
additional and do not qualify.

**Who verifies the reductions?**
An Accredited Carbon Verification Agency (ACVA) — an independent, BEE-accredited
third party. The entity cannot self-certify. Two different ACVAs are used for
validation and verification.

**How long does it take?**
After PDD submission, BEE runs a completeness check (about 10 working days) then
a technical review (30+ days). The full cycle, including a monitoring period,
usually spans a year or more before the first CCCs are issued.

**What happens if the data is wrong?**
Inaccurate quantities or an overstated baseline lead to rejection at
verification, and a false claim can carry regulatory liability. Accurate
measurement of waste quantities and destinations is essential — which is why a
verifiable operational record (grading, weight, GPS) matters.
